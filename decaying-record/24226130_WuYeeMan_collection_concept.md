How Close Is Close Enough?

This collection started from something my granny told me. She explained that a copied key is never exactly the same as the original. Small differences can appear in the teeth and shape. If a copy is used to make another copy, these differences may continue to build up. Each version can move a little further away from the original.

This made me want to compare one original key with its copy. They look very similar and can perform the same function, but they are not completely the same. I recorded them through images, sounds, text, touch, smell and measurement. I found differences in their shape, weight, surface, corrosion, sound and the way they feel when used.

The title "How Close Is Close Enough?" asks when we decide that a copy is “the same” as the original.

The keys also made me think about everyday life. Many days seem repetitive. We sleep, study, work, eat and repeat similar routines. However, no day is exactly the same. Like a copied key, each day carries small changes. As time passes, an older version of ourselves also slowly changes and moves further away.

I placed my granny’s Taishanese recording near the end of the collection, followed by an English translation. Her words were the starting point of this work, so I wanted them to become its ending as well. I also wanted to preserve her voice as a memory between us.

This relates to loss for me. Loss does not always mean that something suddenly disappears. Sometimes it happens slowly through change and time. A recording can keep my granny’s voice, but it cannot keep the whole moment, our relationship, or how I may feel when I listen to it many years later.

In this sense, the collection itself becomes another key: a key that may one day open this memory again.
